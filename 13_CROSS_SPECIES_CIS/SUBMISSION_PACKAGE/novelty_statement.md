# Novelty Statement

**To our knowledge, no prior study has combined these analyses in a
per-subject population-scale human structural-connectome framework with a
pre-registered cross-scale comparison.**

Specifically, the combination — (a) exact per-node removal-based control
impact computed for all 456 nodes in each of 801 subjects, (b) degree- and
strength-matched controls, (c) degree-preserving (Maslov–Sneppen) null
ensembles with per-null exact verification, (d) node-level FDR arbitration of
a degree-independent residual, and (e) a pre-registered fly–human
architectural comparison under no-homology rules — has not been reported by
any verified source (literature matrix: `10_LITERATURE/NOVELTY_MATRIX.md`).

**We do not claim** novelty of the individual methods (node-removal efficiency
analysis, cost thresholding, matched controls, configuration-model nulls, and
FDR control are all established), **nor** a new biological mechanism, nor
anatomical homology, nor a universal brain law.

**Closest neighbors and the distinction:**
- *Kudriavtsev et al. 2026* (bioRxiv): simulated lesions + nulls in ageing —
  matched-**mass** lesion nulls, ageing-decline framing; no per-node
  population residual analysis; no cross-scale bridge. PARTIAL OVERLAP
  (full text pending one manual read; abstract-complete verification on
  file).
- *Alstott 2009 / Crossley 2014*: classic group-average removal analyses —
  no matching, no nulls, no per-subject population statistics.
- *Yueh-Hsin et al. 2024*: multi-node resections, 80 adults — no matched
  controls, no null ensemble, no residual ranking.
- *Controllability (NCT) literature*: a different metric (no node removal).

A dedicated pre-submission sweep of 2025–2026 preprints (E17) and a
re-verification of the closest neighbor (E17a) found no occupation of this
combination; the remaining gate is one manual full-text read of Kudriavtsev
et al. 2026.
